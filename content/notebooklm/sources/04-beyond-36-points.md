# Beyond 36 points: why Ashtakoot matching misses what actually breaks marriages

## What Ashtakoot Milan actually measures

Guna Milan scores eight kootas for a maximum of 36 points. It is the standard
of Indian matchmaking and it is almost universally misunderstood, because
almost nobody looks at what the eight kootas are weighted by.

| Koota | Points | What it compares |
|---|---|---|
| Varna | 1 | Spiritual/work temperament compatibility |
| Vashya | 2 | Mutual influence and control |
| Tara | 3 | Birth-star compatibility, health and fortune |
| Yoni | 4 | Sexual and physical compatibility |
| Graha Maitri | 5 | Friendship between the Moon-sign lords — mental affinity |
| Gana | 6 | Temperament class: Deva, Manushya, Rakshasa |
| Bhakoot | 7 | Moon-sign axis — emotional and financial harmony |
| Nadi | 8 | Constitutional and genetic compatibility |

Two facts follow immediately.

**First: the entire 36 points is calculated from the Moon's nakshatra.** Nothing
else. Not the Lagna. Not the 7th house. Not Venus. Not Mars. Not the dashas.
Two people can score 32 out of 36 and share no other point of compatibility in
their charts, because 32 out of 36 is a statement about two nakshatras and
nothing more.

**Second: Bhakoot and Nadi together are 15 of the 36 points** — 42% of the
score. A single Nadi dosha removes 8 points on its own and can drop an
otherwise excellent match below the conventional 18-point threshold.

## What the score does not look at

A 36-point match is silent on every one of the following:

- **Mangal Dosha.** Not scored at all. It is checked separately, and a couple
  can score 30/36 with an unmitigated Mars mismatch.
- **The 7th house of either chart.** The house of marriage itself contributes
  zero points.
- **Venus.** The natural karaka of relationship contributes zero points.
- **The Navamsa.** The divisional chart *of marriage* contributes zero points.
- **Dasha timing.** Whether either person is entering a period that stresses
  partnership is not considered.
- **The 8th house.** Longevity of the bond, shared finances, and the capacity
  to survive crisis are not scored.
- **Ages, life stage, or whether either has been married before.**

This is the core problem. A system named "compatibility matching" omits the
house of marriage, the planet of love, and the chart of marriage.

## The Bhakoot problem

Bhakoot compares the two Moon signs. Certain axes — 6/8, 9/5, 2/12 — score
zero, and a 6/8 Bhakoot dosha in particular is treated as serious.

But Bhakoot is **cancelled** under well-documented conditions that are routinely
ignored: when both Moon signs are ruled by the same planet; when the Moon-sign
lords are mutual friends; when Graha Maitri scores full marks. A large share of
matches rejected on Bhakoot are cancelled matches that nobody checked.

## The Nadi problem

Nadi dosha — both partners in the same Nadi (Aadi, Madhya or Antya) — costs all
8 points and is treated as the most serious dosha in the system. Its classical
basis is constitutional: two people of the same *prakriti* were held to produce
weaker offspring.

Nadi dosha is also **cancelled** when both have the same Moon sign but different
nakshatras, when both have the same nakshatra but different padas, or when the
Moon-sign lords are identical. And a great many couples rejected on Nadi are
rejected without any of those checks being run.

## What a serious reading checks instead

Ashtakoot is a screen, not a verdict. Past the screen, six things carry more
predictive weight:

1. **Both 7th houses** — sign, occupants, lord placement, and lord strength in
   each chart. This is the house the marriage actually lives in.
2. **Navamsa cross-comparison** — the D9 lagnas, the D9 7th houses, and whether
   each person's 7th lord holds up in the divisional chart.
3. **Mars, properly assessed** — both charts, all reference points, all
   cancellations, and crucially whether the two temperaments can absorb each
   other.
4. **Venus in both charts** — dignity, affliction, combustion. An afflicted
   Venus in either chart predicts more about relationship difficulty than any
   koota.
5. **Dasha overlap** — what period each person enters over the next five and ten
   years, and whether those periods stress the 7th, 8th or 12th.
6. **The 8th house and longevity of the bond** — the part Ashtakoot does not
   touch at all.

## How to say this without dismissing tradition

Guna Milan is not wrong. It is *narrow*, and it was designed for a context —
arranged matches within a community, negotiated quickly, between families who
already knew a great deal about each other — where a fast nakshatra screen was a
reasonable first filter and everything else was known by other means.

Used as the whole answer, it fails in both directions: it rejects sound matches
on a cancelled dosha, and it approves unsound ones because two nakshatras
happened to agree.

> "You scored 29 out of 36, which is good. But the 36 points only compare your
> Moon stars. They don't look at either 7th house, at Venus, at Mars, or at the
> Navamsa. In your case, all four of those are worth looking at before you
> treat 29 as an answer — one of them changes the picture."

That sentence is the pitch. It grants the tradition its place, names precisely
what it leaves out, and points at the work that does the rest.
