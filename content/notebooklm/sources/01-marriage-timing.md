# Marriage timing: how Vedic astrology actually dates a wedding

## The question people ask

"Shaadi kab hogi?" is the single most asked question in Indian astrology. It is
also the one most often answered badly — with a confident year and no method
behind it. The honest answer is that a chart does not name a date. It names
*windows*: periods when the conditions that produce a marriage are switched on
at the same time. Where those windows overlap is where a marriage becomes
likely.

Four independent systems have to agree before a window is worth naming.

## 1. The houses that own the question

The **7th house** is the house of partnership and the legal marriage itself.
Its sign, the planets sitting in it, and the strength and placement of its lord
describe the marriage's basic character and whether it comes early or late.

The **2nd house** governs *kutumba* — family expansion. A marriage that brings
a person into a household registers here.

The **11th house** is fulfilment of desire. Marriage as something wanted and
obtained is an 11th-house event.

The **8th house** governs the shared resources and vulnerabilities of a married
couple, and the durability of the bond.

Activation of 7th, 2nd and 11th together is the classic signature. A period
that lights up only the 7th often produces a relationship, not a wedding.

## 2. The Vimshottari dasha — the clock

Vimshottari dasha divides a life into planetary periods totalling 120 years,
sequenced from the Moon's nakshatra at birth. The *mahadasha* is the governing
period, running from 6 to 20 years. Inside it the *antardasha* subdivides, and
inside that the *pratyantardasha*.

Marriage tends to arrive in a mahadasha–antardasha combination where at least
one of the two planets is a genuine marriage significator: the 7th lord, the
2nd lord, the 11th lord, a planet placed in the 7th house, or Venus — the
natural karaka of marriage for both sexes, and of the wife specifically for a
man. For a woman, Jupiter as karaka of the husband carries the same weight.

A Venus–Jupiter or Jupiter–Venus period in the right chart is among the most
reliable marriage windows in the system.

## 3. Transits — the trigger

Dasha opens the window. Transit walks through it.

**Jupiter** is the primary trigger. Its transit over the natal Moon sign, over
the 7th house from the Moon, or over the natal 7th lord repeatedly coincides
with marriage. Jupiter takes roughly twelve years to circle the zodiac, so it
touches each of these points about once a year at most, and the overlap with a
supporting dasha is what narrows the window.

**Saturn** is the counterweight. Saturn transiting the 7th house frequently
delays rather than delivers, and Sade Sati — Saturn's seven-and-a-half year
passage over the 12th, 1st and 2nd from the natal Moon — is a well-known period
of postponement. Saturn does not prevent marriage. It slows it and it raises
the seriousness of the choice.

**Rahu** transiting the 7th can produce a sudden or unconventional match,
often one that crosses a boundary the family did not expect.

## 4. Confirmation: Navamsa, Upapada, and the KP sub-lord

Three cross-checks separate a real window from a coincidence.

**The Navamsa (D9)** is the divisional chart of marriage. A 7th lord that is
strong in the birth chart but debilitated in D9 promises more than it delivers.
The reverse — weak in D1, strong in D9 — is *vargottama*-adjacent and often
produces a marriage better than the birth chart suggested.

**Upapada Lagna (UL)** is the Jaimini point for marriage. The sign of the UL,
its lord, and the 2nd house from it describe the spouse and the marriage's
sustainability.

**The KP 7th cusp sub-lord** is the most precise single indicator in the
system. In Krishnamurti Paddhati, the sub-lord of the 7th cusp decides whether
marriage is promised at all. If that sub-lord signifies the 7th, 2nd or 11th
houses, marriage is promised and timing becomes a question of *when*. If it
signifies the 6th, 10th or 12th, the chart is indicating denial, separation or
long delay — and no amount of favourable transit overrides it.

## How to say this honestly

A good reading names a range, states the reasoning, and admits the limit:

> "Two windows stand out. The first is Venus–Jupiter, roughly late 2027 to
> early 2029, with Jupiter transiting your 7th from the Moon through most of
> it. The second opens in 2031. Your 7th cusp sub-lord signifies the 2nd and
> 11th, so marriage is promised — this is a question of when, not whether. The
> first window is the stronger of the two."

What it does not do is name a date. Anyone naming a date is guessing.

## Common misconceptions worth correcting

- **"Late marriage means a bad marriage."** Saturn's involvement usually means
  later and more deliberate, and Saturn-timed marriages are among the most
  durable in the system.
- **"Manglik means no marriage."** It does not. See the Mangal Dosha document.
- **"My friend has the same rashi so we'll marry at the same time."** Rashi is
  the Moon's sign — one factor of the dozen above. Two people share it and
  share nothing else.
