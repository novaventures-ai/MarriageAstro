# How you love: attachment temperament in a birth chart

## What this describes, and what it refuses to

This reading describes *how a person attaches* — what they need in order to feel
secure, how they behave when they do not, and what a partner should expect from
them emotionally.

It does **not** count relationships. It cannot, and any system claiming to is
selling something. A chart shows disposition, not history: two people with an
identical signature can have lived entirely different lives, because what a
person does with a temperament is theirs to decide. Attachment style is about
*how* someone loves, not how much of a past they have.

That distinction matters most in arranged matching, where a reading is handed
to a family and read as a character verdict. A description of temperament is
useful there. A number implying history is character assassination with a
horoscope attached.

## The indicators

**The 5th house** governs romance, attraction and the capacity for
infatuation. A loaded 5th — multiple planets, especially Venus, Mars or Rahu —
describes someone who falls hard and falls readily. An empty or malefic-only
5th describes someone slow to open.

**Venus** is the core indicator. Its sign describes what the person finds
lovable; its dignity describes how steadily they can hold a bond. Venus
exalted in Pisces or in its own signs gives an easy, generous capacity for
affection. Venus debilitated in Virgo gives someone who loves through analysis
and service, often critically, and who struggles to simply receive affection.
Venus combust — too close to the Sun — describes someone whose identity
overwhelms their affection: they love, but the self keeps getting in the way.

**The Moon** is the attachment system itself. The Moon's sign and nakshatra
describe what a person needs to feel safe. A Moon in Cancer or Pisces needs
emotional reassurance, frequently and explicitly. A Moon in Capricorn or
Aquarius needs space and demonstrated reliability, and reads constant
reassurance as pressure. A waning or afflicted Moon describes someone who needs
more reassurance than they will ever ask for.

**Rahu touching the 5th or 7th** describes intensity and a pull toward the
unfamiliar — attraction that crosses boundaries the person expected to hold.

**Mars in the 5th or 7th** describes physical directness and impatience in
affection.

**Saturn in the 5th or 7th** describes caution, slowness to open, and loyalty
once open. Saturn's reserve is frequently mistaken for coldness; it is usually
fear of the cost of being wrong.

## The five temperaments

The indicators resolve into five bands. None is better than the others, and any
presentation that colours one green and another red has misunderstood the
reading.

**Intense.** Attaches quickly and completely. Feels love as an overwhelming
state rather than a gradual one. Needs a great deal of contact and reassurance,
and experiences distance as rejection. At its best: wholehearted, unguarded,
genuinely present. At its cost: consuming, and prone to reading ordinary
silence as withdrawal. *A partner should expect:* to be needed visibly, and to
have to say things out loud that they assumed were obvious.

**Warm.** Opens readily and generously. Enjoys affection without being consumed
by it, and gives it easily. At its best: easy to be close to. At its cost: can
avoid necessary conflict to keep the warmth intact. *A partner should expect:*
affection offered freely, and some difficulty getting hard conversations
started.

**Balanced.** Attaches steadily, neither quickly nor reluctantly. Holds their
own centre inside a relationship. At its best: reliable and self-possessed. At
its cost: can read as unbothered by someone who needs more visible
reassurance. *A partner should expect:* consistency rather than intensity.

**Selective.** Opens slowly and to few people. Tests before trusting, and
commits thoroughly once the testing is done. At its best: deeply loyal and
unusually deliberate. At its cost: the testing period is real and can feel like
rejection to someone waiting it out. *A partner should expect:* to earn it, and
then to have it permanently.

**Private.** Keeps the inner life separate even inside a close relationship.
Needs solitude as a condition of intimacy rather than a retreat from it. At its
best: self-sufficient, undemanding, steady. At its cost: a partner can feel
shut out of a room they did not know existed. *A partner should expect:* to be
told less than they want, and to have to ask directly.

## What a partner actually wants to know

In a real matching conversation, temperament is not the live question. The live
question is: **is this person ready to be here now, and will they be loyal
going forward?**

That question is answerable, and it is answered elsewhere in the chart — by the
2nd house from the Upapada, by the condition of the 7th lord in the Navamsa, by
Jupiter's relationship to the 7th, by Saturn's capacity for commitment, and by
which dasha the person is entering. Those indicators speak to present readiness
and future conduct.

A temperament reading describes the *shape* of someone's love. It does not, and
should not, pretend to audit their past.

## How to present it

Name the band, name the placements that produce it, describe what a partner
should expect, and mark the limit explicitly:

> "Your Moon is in Capricorn and Saturn aspects your 7th. You read Selective:
> slow to open, thorough about testing, and very hard to shift once you have
> decided. Someone close to you should expect to earn it and then to have it
> permanently. This describes how you attach — not how much of a past you
> have."

That last clause is not a disclaimer. It is the part that keeps the reading
honest.
